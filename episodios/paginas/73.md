## Summary

In this episode of the “Fronteiras da Engenharia de Software” (Frontiers of Software Engineering) podcast, Adolfo Neto and Lidiany Cerqueira interviewed Dr. Simone Eringfeld, the author of the “Podcasting as a Research Method: A Practical Guide” book.  In her book, Simone “explores podcasting as a powerful tool for data collection and knowledge production”. We talked about the three approaches discussed in the book: podcasting as participatory action research, sonic elicitation and podcast ethnography.

Simone—a researcher, expedition guide, field recordist, podcaster, sound artist, and Gates Cambridge Scholar currently pursuing a PhD in Polar Studies at the University of Cambridge—shared insights into how podcasts are evolving beyond simple research dissemination into rigorous methodological tools.

During the conversation, we explored:

Defining the medium: What constitutes a podcast and how its flexible nature supports multimedia academic work.

Practical methodologies: How researchers, including those in software engineering, can adopt sonic elicitation (even without producing their own show), participatory action research (inspired by Simone's Cambridge Quaranchats project), and podcast ethnography (which she is using for her polar studies dissertation).

Behind the scenes of academic podcasting: The origin story of her book, why she chose to write a text rather than produce a meta-podcast on the topic, and strategies for navigating funding challenges compared to traditional academic outputs.

Ethics and validity: How to mitigate researcher bias and social desirability bias to protect data validity when recording participants.

The role of AI: The difference between using AI as a helpful assistant (such as for transcription) versus generating full AI episodes from documents—including the risks of unnoticed errors and the value of keeping podcasts human-centered.
